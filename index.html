The problem

Millions of traders keep their books in their heads.

Across Ghana's markets, most traders, most of them women, track sales from memory or in exercise books. At the end of a busy day nobody knows exactly what sold, what is owed, or whether the day made money.

Without records, traders are shut out of credit and can't see how their business is really doing.

How it works

She sells as she always has. We keep the books.

01

Morning

Phone in the apron

She keeps her phone in her apron pocket, or pairs a Bluetooth headset or earpiece.

02

All day

It listens through the day

Voice Ledger picks up her conversations with customers as she sells, in the language she already speaks, including mixed Twi and English and local units like "olonka" and "bag".

03

Every sale

Every sale captured

  • Item
  • Quantity
  • Price
  • Buyer or debtor

The built-in intelligence recognises each sale and writes it down, including who bought it or who owes.

04

Anytime

Check anytime

She opens the app whenever she likes to see today's total, confirm each sale was recorded right, and correct anything.

05

Closing time

End-of-day summary

A plain-language summary in her own language: total sales, best sellers, money owed, and spending.

What makes it different

Built for the market, not the office.

Hands-free

No typing, no buttons while serving customers.

Speaks Ghanaian languages

Twi, Ga, Ewe, and English, including code-switching.

Offline first

Works in the market without data. On-device speech and speaker identification (TensorFlow Lite), with encrypted local storage.

The trader is in control

She reviews and edits every entry. Recordings stay on her phone.

Export when needed

CSV, PDF, and Excel records she can show a bank, susu group, or microfinance lender.

The intelligence

Built with Claude, to understand how markets really talk.

Claude turns messy, code-switched market conversations into structured sales records.

It writes daily and weekly business insights in the trader's own language, and powers a simple assistant she can just ask.

"How much did I make this week?"

"Who still owes me?"
